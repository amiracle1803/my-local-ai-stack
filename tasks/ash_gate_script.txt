The Ash Gate

Mira stood before the iron gate of the old shrine, rain soaking through her thin gray cloak. She was a young woman with short black hair and a scar across her left cheek, carrying only a lantern and a knife. Behind her, the forest burned with a low orange glow.

"You should not be here," said Ryo. He stepped out from beneath the gate's arch, an older swordsman with a gray beard, wearing a tattered blue coat. His hand rested on the hilt of a chipped katana.

"The fire took my village," Mira said. Her voice did not shake. "The Ash Spirit lives beyond this gate. I am going to end it."

"The gate only opens for the desperate," Ryo replied. He looked her up and down, then gave a tired laugh. "You will do, girl. Stay behind me."

He drew his blade and pressed it against the seal carved into the iron. The gate groaned and swung inward, revealing a courtyard full of floating cinders that glowed like fireflies.

Inside, the heat was overwhelming. At the center of the courtyard knelt a figure made of smoke and embers — the Ash Spirit, vast and formless, its eyes two points of white flame.

"Mortal children," the Spirit whispered, and its voice was the crackle of a dying fire. "Why do you come to the one who burns?"

"You took my family," Mira shouted, raising her lantern. "You burn everything you touch."

The Spirit's flames flickered, dimming for a moment. "I burn because I am alone," it said. "Everything I touch turns to ash. I did not choose this."

Mira lowered her lantern slowly. Ryo glanced at her but said nothing, his blade still ready.

"Then let me sit with you," Mira said. She walked forward and sat on the scorched stones, just out of reach of the flames. "Tell me your name. Nobody burns who is remembered."

The Ash Spirit stared at her for a long time. The cinders in the air grew still, one by one, like snow settling. "My name," it said at last, "was En."

By dawn the courtyard had cooled. The gate stood open. Mira walked out with Ryo beside her, and behind them, for the first time in a hundred years, no fire followed.
